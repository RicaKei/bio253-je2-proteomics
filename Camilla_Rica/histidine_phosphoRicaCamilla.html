05 – Does S. aureus phosphorylate histidine?

Bio253 HS2026 · STY vs STYH search, judged conservatively

Claim to test: Prust et al. 2021 (Mol Cell Proteomics 20:100034) report that ~8 % of all S. aureus phospho-sites are on histidine (pHis). Can we see pHis in our data – and how sure can we be?

The raw files were searched twice:

  • STY – phosphorylation allowed on Ser, Thr, Tyr
  • STYH – additionally on His
ImportantBe sceptical – on purpose
  • pHis is acid-labile. Our workflow (Fe-IMAC enrichment, acidic LC) is not pHis-friendly, so many pHis may be lost – and the ones we “see” need extra scrutiny.
  • The main source of false pHis is mis-localisation: if a peptide also contains S, T or Y, the search engine may put the phosphate on H although it sits on a neighbour – or the other way round.
  • Allowing H as an extra acceptor makes the search space bigger, which by itself creates some extra (false) hits.

We therefore treat pHis very conservatively. You will build evidence tiers yourself.

3b. Is there an alternative acceptor on the peptide?

If the peptide contains no S, T or Y at all, the phosphate cannot be mis-placed onto a neighbouring S/T/Y. That is the strongest kind of evidence we can get from the search alone. A Peptide entry can contain several sequence variants separated by ; – we take the variant with the fewest S/T/Y.

6. Your task: build confidence tiers

Combine the evidence into tiers, from “strongest candidate” to “unreliable”. Suggested criteria (you decide the order and thresholds – and justify them!):

evidence column
no alternative S/T/Y acceptor on the peptide no_alt_acceptor
well localised best_loc_prob >= 0.75
reproducible: ≥ 2 of 3 replicates in some group max_reps_in_group >= 2
peptide not explained by the STY search !seen_in_STY_search

Write your tiers below (set eval: true once you filled in the conditions). Number tiers 1 (strongest) to 6 (weakest) and start every label with the number.

my_tiers <- his |>
  mutate(
    tier = case_when(
      no_alt_acceptor &
        best_loc_prob >= 0.75 &
        max_reps_in_group >= 2 &
        !seen_in_STY_search ~ "1 strongest",
      best_loc_prob >= 0.75 &
        max_reps_in_group >= 2 &
        !seen_in_STY_search ~ "2 very strong",

        best_loc_prob >= 0.75 ~ "3 strong",

7. Real or artefact? A decision framework

For each candidate, go through this list and write your verdict in your lab notebook.

  1. Chemistry: acid-labile pHis + acidic workflow → is it plausible the site survived?
  2. Localisation: LP ≥ 0.75? Any S/T/Y on the peptide? Same peptide explained by STY?
  3. Reproducibility: seen in ≥ 2/3 replicates of a group? In more than one group?
  4. Biology: is the protein a known His-phosphorylated protein? Is the His conserved?
  5. Spectra: (beyond this course, but name it) inspect the MS2 spectra – are there fragment ions that pin the phosphate to H? pHis shows characteristic neutral losses (e.g. −80, −98, −116 Da).
  6. Orthogonal validation: pHis-specific antibodies, non-acidic enrichment, mutation of the His.

Discussion: after this analysis, how would you phrase our evidence for (or against) the claim of Prust et al.? Distinguish “we found no evidence” from “we found evidence of absence”.
